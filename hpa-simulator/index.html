glossary — how an HPA decides, and why the two fleets diverge

Stabilization windows (behavior.scaleUp/scaleDown.stabilizationWindowSeconds): every few seconds the HPA computes a desired pod count; the windows remember those recent answers. The scaleUp window scans the last N seconds and keeps the lowest answer — the result can never land lower than it. The scaleDown window keeps the highest — the result can never land higher. The answer itself starts at your current count and gets clamped between those two; today's fresh reading only joins the memory pool. That's why a fleet lags a burst by a minute and refuses to shrink for five: one old busy (or quiet) moment inside the window pins the range.

Rate policies (behavior.*.policies): the speed limit on top of the wish — e.g. +2 pods or +50% per 60s going up, −1 pod or −25% going down. selectPolicy: Max picks the larger change, Min the smaller; min/maxReplicas clamp the result.

Fleet A — External/Value: desired = ceil(pods × wait ÷ aim). Queue wait is ONE global number, identical on every exporter — HPA multiplies it by the pod count, so the recommendation explodes (wait 18s × 5 pods = “want 93”).

Fleet A — External/AverageValue: desired = ceil(Σ wait ÷ aim) over a per-pod series that doesn't exist — it divides by pod counts that never contributed to the measurement.

Fleet B — Pods/Utilization: slot fullness is genuinely per-pod, so the math works — its cost is that pod-busyness is blind to how deep the queue is.
